FAQ

Things people ask Sam.

If yours is not here, the chat in the corner knows the same answers and the contact page reaches Sam.

Local or API

What does "local" actually mean?

An open model (Qwen 3.8 is our current pick) running on a small box we set up in your office, wired to your network and nothing else. Your documents are indexed on that box. Questions and answers never leave the building, and there is no per-token bill.

When would we use a frontier model instead?

When the task needs the very top of the reasoning scale: long legal analysis, hard research, unusual code. We route those requests through our guard to Fable 5.1, Astra, Grok 4.6 or whichever model won on your tasks. Chat and everyday work stay local.

Can we mix both?

That is the normal setup. The local model answers by default; the guarded path is a per-task switch, not a different product.

Who picks the model?

We do, from evidence. Before your data touches anything we run the whole field against your real tasks and show you the scorecard. You can change the pick any time; the box does not care which model is loaded.

Security

What is "our guard"?

Two steps on every API call. First a screen for prompt injection, so a document your staff paste cannot instruct the model to leak something. Then a small local model hashes names, account numbers, addresses and anything that looks personal before the request leaves the building. The frontier model sees tokens like PERSON_7; the answer comes back and the same local model puts the real details back on your screen.

What does the provider see?

An anonymised request and nothing about who asked. We keep no copy of the plaintext on the API side; the mapping between hash and detail never leaves your box.

Can we audit it?

Yes. Every guarded request writes a local log line: what was hashed, what was screened, which model answered, how long it took. Your security team can read it; we cannot.

Where does the local box sit?

Under a desk, in a closet, in your rack. It needs power and one network cable. Remote staff reach it through your existing VPN.

Fine-tuning

What does fine-tuning actually change?

A small adapter, about 0.4% of the weights, learns your patterns: your vocabulary, your formats, your rules, the answers your people would sign. The base model stays untouched, so we keep one adapter per team and can roll any of them back in a click. The full picture is on the Custom Solutions page.

How long does a fine-tune take?

Four weeks for one task: a week to gather the examples, a week to refine and grade them, a week to train and prove the result against the base model and the best frontier model, and a week to ship and watch it. The training itself is a few hours on the box.

Does our data leave the building to train?

No. The examples are pulled onto the box, deduplicated and hashed there, and the adapter trains on the same hardware. Nothing is uploaded anywhere at any step.

Will it know things that change every day?

That is the index's job, not the adapter's. Fine-tuning teaches habits; the index on the box supplies today's facts. The tuned model reads it like every other model does.

Pricing and process

How is it priced?

A local build is a one-time hardware cost plus a flat monthly support fee, with no usage metering. Guarded API access is billed at the provider's published token price plus our guard, itemised. We quote after a short call.

How long from first call to working box?

About two weeks. Most of that is indexing your documents and running the model scorecard on your tasks. The install itself is a morning: we carry the box in, plug it in, and leave when your first answer comes back.

What happens overnight?

Every answer is graded overnight by people who read them; every miss becomes the next day's training example. You get a scored report in your inbox each morning.

Can we leave?

The box is yours, the model is open, the index is in a standard format. If you stop paying support, everything keeps working; you just stop getting the overnight grading and updates.
