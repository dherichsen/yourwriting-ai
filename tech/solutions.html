How a fine-tune happens.

Five steps, four weeks. The box does the work; your people do the judging.

01 / 05GATHER
  1. 01 · GATHER

    Your examples, pulled from where they already live.

    Support tickets, contracts, past replies, call notes, the spreadsheet everyone actually uses. We pull them from your systems onto the box in your building. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and nothing is typed in by hand.

  2. 02 · REFINE

    Deduplicated, redacted, graded by your people.

    Duplicates and near-duplicates drop out. Names, account numbers and anything personal are hashed before a single example is used. Then two or three of your best people grade a sample, so the model learns from answers you would sign.

  3. 03 · TUNE

    A thin adapter learns. The base model stays intact.

    We do not retrain the whole model. A small adapter, about 0.4% of the weights, learns your patterns over a few hours on the box. The base stays untouched, which is why we can keep one adapter per team and roll any of them back in a click.

  4. 04 · PROVE

    Scored against the same tasks, before and after.

    The held-out examples nobody trained on become the exam. Base model, tuned model and the best guarded frontier model are graded side by side by the same people, blind. You see the number before anything ships.

  5. 05 · SHIP

    Live on the box in under a minute. Roll back in one click.

    The adapter drops onto the local model; the old version stays on disk. From then on the overnight grading keeps feeding it: every miss your people mark becomes the next week's training example.

Four weeks, one task.

A pilot is scoped to one job the model should do well. Narrow on purpose: it is the fastest way to a number you can trust.

  1. WEEK 1Audit and gather

    We pick one task with you, find where the examples live and pull them onto the box.

  2. WEEK 2Refine and grade

    Dedupe, hash, and three hours of grading from the people who know a good answer.

  3. WEEK 3Tune and prove

    Train the adapter, score it blind against base and frontier on the held-out set.

  4. WEEK 4Ship and watch

    Live on the box. Overnight grading starts feeding the next version.

WHAT YOU BRING

  • One owner for the task, who can say what a good answer is.
  • Two or three graders for about three hours in week two.
  • Access to the systems where the examples live.

WHAT YOU KEEP

  • The adapter, on your box, in a standard format.
  • The eval set and the scorecard, base against tuned against frontier.
  • The rollback: every version stays on disk.

Where we'll tell you no.

  • It won't learn today's news.

    Fine-tuning teaches habits, not facts that change daily. Yesterday's prices and this morning's tickets come from the index on the box, which the tuned model reads like everyone else.

  • It won't turn a small model into a frontier one.

    A tuned Qwen 3.8 beats Fable on your task because it has seen your task. Ask it something it has never seen and it is Qwen 3.8 again. That is what the guarded path is for.

  • It can't learn a task nobody can grade.

    If your people cannot agree on what a good answer looks like, the model cannot either. We find that out in week one, and we will say so rather than train on noise.
